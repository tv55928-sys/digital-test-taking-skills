      A line is represented by the equation y = 6x. The slope doubles, and the y-intercept decreases by 5. What is the equation of the new line?

      Your turn: use your whiteboard

      A line is represented by y = −2x + 7. The slope triples, and the y-intercept increases by 3. What is the new equation?

      Show the solution

      m = −2, b = 7

      triples → −2 × 3 = −6

      increases by 3 → 7 + 3 = 10

      y = −6x + 10

      Station 4 of 10

      Use the anchor charts in our room

      Our anchor charts stay on the classroom walls during the test. If you forget a word or a rule, look up at the right chart. Learn where each chart is before test day.

      SEL Identifying resources and supports that aid perseverance

      Which chart would help?

      Read each question. Choose the anchor chart you would look at.

      How to use a chart during the test

      1. Find the key word in the question, for example slope.
      2. Look up at the chart for that word.
      3. Look back at the question and use the rule on your paper.

      Make it a habit now. Every time you practise, look up at a chart at least once.

      Station 5 of 10

      Check your thinking with Desmos

      Desmos is a free graphing calculator. You can use a graphing tool on the test if you use it often in class. Use Desmos to check your paper work. Do the thinking yourself first.

      SEL Developing critical and creative mathematical thinkingOpen Desmos ↗

      Six Desmos moves for this unit

      1. Graph an equation

        Type y=12x-5. Use - for minus. Do not type the × sign: 12x means 12 times x.

      2. Read the y-intercept

        Click the line. Grey points appear. Click the point on the y-axis to see (0, b).

      3. Make a table

        Press +, then table. Type the x and y values. Do the points make a straight line? Then it is linear.

      4. Find the equation from a table

        Under the table, type y_1~mx_1+b. Desmos shows m and b. Use this to check, not to replace your work.

      5. See what m and b do

        Type y=mx+b and click add slider for m and b. Move them and watch the line.

      6. Find where lines meet

        Type two equations. Click the point where they cross to see its coordinates.

      Station 6 of 10

      Break down the question

      EQAO questions use a lot of words. You do not need to know every word. You need the key words. Key words tell you what the question is asking and what math to do.

      SEL Developing critical and creative mathematical thinking

      Five steps to break down a question

      1. Read or listenRead it two times. Use the read-aloud tool if you want.
      2. Find the questionThe last sentence is usually the question. What are they asking for?
      3. Highlight key wordsslope, y-intercept, constant, doubles, decreases by…
      4. Circle numbers and unitsL, min, $, per month
      5. PredictGuess the answer before you read the choices.

      Practise with the highlighter

      Click the key words in each question. Then press Check my highlights.

      How the test works

      2sessions: Session A and Session B
      2stages in each session, 4 stages in total
      ~60 minfor each session (two stages)
      AdaptiveYour Stage 1 work decides if Stage 2 is easier or harder.
      • You can flag a question to come back to it later.
      • Before you submit a stage, the test shows you questions that are flagged or blank.
      • After you submit a stage, you cannot go back to it.
      • Your teacher can answer questions about the computer and the tools. Your teacher cannot help with the math.
      • You can use headphones, paper, a pencil, a calculator, the EQAO Formula Sheet and the English–French glossary.

        Tips for success

        You have the skills. Here is a short list to remember. Read it again the day before the test.

        SEL Developing a healthy mathematical identity

        Before the test

        • Sleep well and eat breakfast.
        • Charge your laptop. Bring headphones.
        • Set up your desk the way you practised.
        • Fold your paper into boxes and number them.
        • Find the anchor charts in the room.
        • Do box breathing before you press Start.

        During the test

        • Read every question two times, or listen to it.
        • Highlight the key words. Find the question.
        • Solve on paper first. Then click.
        • "Select the TWO" means choose exactly two.
        • Check your answer with Desmos or by working backwards.
        • Answer every question. A blank question gets no marks.

        When you are stuck

        • Choose your best answer and flag it.
        • Cross out choices that cannot be right.
        • Try another way: a table, a graph, or Desmos.
        • Look up at an anchor chart.
        • Breathe. Say: "I can't do this yet."

        Before you submit a stage

        • Open the review screen.
        • Go back to every flagged question.
        • Make sure there are no blank questions.
        • Check "Select the TWO" questions have two answers.
        • Then submit. You cannot go back after.

        For the teacher

        Teacher notes

        This module supports English language learners in MTH1W as they prepare for the EQAO Grade 9 Assessment of Mathematics. It uses the C4 Characteristics of Relations unit (C4.1, C4.4) as the math context and teaches the AA1 social-emotional learning skills explicitly.

        Curriculum connections

        C4.1 Characteristics of Linear and Non-Linear Relations: compare characteristics of graphs, tables of values, and equations of linear and non-linear relations

        C4.4 Characteristics of Linear and Non-Linear Relations: determine the equations of lines from graphs, tables of values, and concrete representations of linear relations by making connections between rates of change and slopes, and between initial values and y-intercepts, and use these equations to solve problems

        AA1 Social-Emotional Learning Skills: develop and explore a variety of social-emotional learning skills in a context that supports and reflects this learning in connection with the expectations across all other strands

        AA1 is taught but not assessed, evaluated, or reported. EQAO does not measure the SEL strand.

        Suggested flow (two 75-minute periods)

        1. Day 1: Stations 1–4 together. Model the whiteboard routine; students solve the "Your turn" on vertical whiteboards in pairs.
        2. Day 1: Station 5 in Desmos on laptops.
        3. Day 2: Stations 6–8, then Station 9 under test-like conditions (paper, headphones, quiet).
        4. Day 2: Debrief Stage 1 results using the provincial data below; finish with Station 10.

        ELL supports built in

        • Short sentences and a read-aloud button on every station (uses the device's voice).
        • Tap-to-define key words and a word wall with a space for students' first language.
        • Sentence frames for talk and reflection.
        • Student notes and answers are saved only in each student's browser. Nothing is sent to you.

        Notes on test rules

        • Structure, flagging, and "cannot return after submitting a stage" come from EQAO's administration script.
        • Permitted items (paper, pencil, ruler, headphones, scientific or graphing calculator, printed Formula Sheet and glossaries, graphing applications students use regularly) and supports for all students (additional time, quiet setting, breaks) come from the EQAO knowledge base.
        • EQAO states that applications containing a glossary are not permitted and that classroom displays do not need to be altered for the assessment. Confirm phone and device rules with your school's administration.
        • The mock test screen is an approximation built for practice. Have students also try EQAO's official sample test.
